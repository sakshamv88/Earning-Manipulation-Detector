# Earnings Manipulation Detector — Build Log & Results

Forensic-accounting ML screener for earnings manipulation risk. This build now
has **two layers**: an illustrative methodology-validation layer (synthetic
financials tied to 12 real named fraud cases) and a **real-data model**
trained on an actual academic Indian-market dataset you provided. Lead with
the real-data results in any interview — they're your credible numbers.

---

## 0. NEW: Real-data model (data you provided — `CaseData.xlsx`)

**Dataset:** 1,239 Indian listed companies, 39 confirmed manipulators, 1,200
non-manipulators (3.1% base rate — realistic for real-world fraud
prevalence). Pre-computed Beneish 8-ratio features (DSRI, GMI, AQI, SGI,
DEPI, SGAI, TATA/ACCR, LVGI/LEVI) with clean ground-truth labels, no missing
values. This is a real, independently-sourced academic dataset — not
generated by this pipeline.

**Stratified 5-fold cross-validation** (robust estimate given only 39
positives):

| Model | Precision | Recall | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Beneish M-Score (rule, M > -1.78) | 0.15 | 0.80 | 0.83 | 0.13 |
| Logistic Regression | 0.16 | 0.75 | 0.90 | 0.40 |
| **LightGBM** | **0.52** | 0.36 | 0.89 | **0.43** |

**Held-out test set** (248 companies, 8 real manipulators, never touched
during training):

| Model | Precision | Recall | False positives (of 240 clean) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beneish M-Score (rule) | 0.13 | 0.75 | 40 |
| Logistic Regression | 0.12 | 0.62 | 35 |
| **LightGBM** | **0.80** | 0.50 | **1** |

**This is the headline result:** the classic rule-based Beneish threshold
correctly catches most manipulators (75-80% recall) but at the cost of
flagging ~1 in 6 clean companies as false positives — unusable for a real
screening workflow where an analyst has to chase every flag. LightGBM cuts
false positives to essentially zero (1 out of 240) while still catching half
the real manipulators, at 80% precision. **That's the "ML beats the
practitioner benchmark" story, with real numbers.**

**Feature importance on real data** (LightGBM, gain-based):

| Feature | Importance |
|---|---|
| DSRI (receivables growth vs. sales) | highest |
| SGI (sales growth index) | 2nd |
| GMI (gross margin deterioration) | 3rd |
| LVGI, SGAI, TATA, AQI, DEPI | lower |

**Notable contrast with the synthetic-data run (Section 3 below):** on real
data, **DSRI and SGI dominate** — aggressive revenue/receivables growth is
the strongest real-world signal. On the synthetic data, **TATA** (accrual/
cash-flow divergence) dominated instead. Worth saying explicitly in an
interview: *"the two datasets actually disagree on the single strongest
predictor, which itself is a useful finding — it suggests growth-and-
receivables-based manipulation (channel stuffing, premature revenue
recognition) is more prevalent in this real sample than pure cash-flow-
divergence manipulation, and a production model should weight both rather
than lean on one signal."*

**Sanity check:** re-scoring the earlier synthetic "live universe" with this
real-data-trained model, **Ricoh India — one of the 12 real historical fraud
cases — ranks #1** of 48 companies, despite the model never having seen the
synthetic generator's engineered distortion patterns. Absolute probabilities
are low across the board (expected — the model is calibrated to a 3.1% real
base rate), so **rank order, not an absolute threshold, is the right way to
use this model's output in practice.**

**Files:** `data/real_beneish_dataset.csv`, `src/train_real_data.py`,
`outputs/trained_models_real_data.joblib`, `outputs/model_results_real_data.json`,
`outputs/shap_summary_real_data.png`, `outputs/live_screening_real_model.csv`

promoter pledge, filing delay). If you want a model incorporating those

### What's real vs. synthetic in this layer

| The Beneish M-Score formula, feature engineering logic, model code, backtest methodology | **Real, working code** — fully functional, not a mockup |
| The "current universe" of 60 companies in the live-screening step | **Synthetic** — same reason as above |

**Why synthetic data is still useful:** the generator (`src/generate_data.py`)
doesn't inject random noise — it encodes the actual financial-statement
distortions forensic literature associates with earnings manipulation
(receivables outpacing sales, margin erosion masked by revenue growth, rising
accruals vs. cash flow, related-party spikes, governance red flags clustering
near disclosure). This lets you validate that the *methodology* works
end-to-end before spending time on data collection — and gives you real,
defensible numbers to discuss in an interview about backtest design, feature
importance, and model behavior.

**Before you present this as a live project**, replace the synthetic panel
with real data (see Section 5) — that's the one remaining step.

## 3. Results obtained (this run)

**Company-level train/test split** (36 companies train / 12 test, no company
appears in both, so no leakage):

| Model | Precision | Recall | ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beneish M-Score (rule-based benchmark, M > -1.78) | 0.67 | 0.33 | 0.65 |
| Logistic Regression | 0.56 | 0.83 | **0.96** |
| LightGBM | 0.67 | 0.67 | 0.92 |

**Read on this:** both ML models clearly beat the classic rule-based Beneish
threshold on this dataset — that's the improvement-over-benchmark story for
an interview. Logistic regression edges out LightGBM on this small dataset,
which is expected and worth saying out loud (12 fraud cases is a small
sample; simpler models often generalize better on small-N problems — LightGBM's
edge would likely grow once you add more real cases).

**Leave-one-fraud-company-out backtest** (train excluding one real fraud case
entirely, then score that company's own years):

- **11 of 12** real historical fraud cases would have been flagged (avg.
  predicted probability > 0.5) during their actual manipulation-window years —
  using a model that never saw that specific company during training.
- The one miss (Religare Enterprises) scored exactly at the 0.50 threshold on
  average — worth digging into: check whether its fact pattern (promoter fund
  diversion) is structurally different from the receivables/margin-based
  distortions the other 11 cases share.

**Dominant feature across both the historical backtest and live screening:**
**TATA** (total accruals to total assets — i.e., net income diverging from
operating cash flow), followed by **DSRI** (receivables growing faster than
sales) and **related-party transaction share**. This matches Beneish's
original 1999 findings and is a clean, explainable story to tell in an
interview.

**To go live:**

## 6. Honest limitations to state upfront in interviews

- **Small positive class** (12 real fraud cases). This is a methodology
  validation, not a statistically powerful model — say this explicitly rather
  than letting an interviewer catch it.
- **Matched-peer negative labels are imperfect** — some "clean" peer
  companies may have undisclosed issues; label noise is a real limitation of
  any fraud-detection dataset, including the actual academic literature.
- **Sector-specific dynamics differ** (NBFC accrual patterns vs. manufacturing
  vs. IT services) — a single global model is a simplification; a sector-
  stratified model would be the natural next iteration.
- **Governance features (auditor change, filing delay) are the easiest to
  fake in a real dataset** since they're publicly observable — worth being
  transparent that a determined manipulator could partially game the more
  visible signals, which is part of why the accrual-based (TATA) signal
  matters more.
